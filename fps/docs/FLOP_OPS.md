# FLOP OPS: creative bible

**A legitimate modern military shooter trapped inside an increasingly stupid universe.**

Flop Ops looks and presents itself like a serious, high-budget military shooter: cinematic
lighting, a crisp CoD-style HUD, heavy gunplay, helicopters, armour, explosions. The comedy
comes from the contrast between how seriously the game treats everything and how stupid or
chaotic the events become. It is never a cartoon. The world is photoreal; the plans are idiotic.

## Characters

- **Doug**: the player. An absurdly capable special-operations soldier who stays calm and
  barely reacts even when everything is falling apart. His lines are flat, short and
  practical ("Noted." / "That's a lot of helicopter." / "I'll allow it."). He never shouts,
  never winks at the camera.
- **Extra Special Forces (ESF)**: Doug's unit. Sent on increasingly dangerous missions with
  ridiculous plans and maximum confidence. Briefings are delivered by **Command** in perfect
  military cadence, and the plans are terrible ("Phase two: Doug.").
- **The enemy**: a real-looking hostile force (tactical kit, rifles, cover, flanking) whose
  callsigns, radio chatter and deaths are quietly absurd.

## Tone rules

1. Presentation is 100% serious: typography, HUD, grading, audio mix, menus.
2. Humour lives in writing (mission names, objectives, killfeed, barks, loading tips),
   situations and physics (floppy ragdolls, props that go flying, over-the-top chain
   reactions). Deadpan beats slapstick.
3. No parody logos, no real brands, no memes, no fourth-wall breaks. It's not a spoof of CoD;
   it's its own franchise that happens to be ridiculous.
4. Violence stays at CoD level. Goofy deaths are about ragdoll physics and timing, not gore.

## Pillars

- **Fast, satisfying FPS**: tight gunplay, CoD movement (sprint, slide, mantle, lean), readable enemies.
- **Physics chaos**: ragdolls that launch and flop, explosions with too much force, dynamic props.
- **Cinematic set pieces**: helicopters, missiles, armoured vehicles, breach-and-clear raids.
- **Dry humour**: the mission is going catastrophically wrong and everyone acts like it's fine.

## Current build scope

The game is built on Nerd of Duty / Claude of Duty (MIT, see `LICENSE`). The first playable
mission reuses its wave-defence loop, re-themed as an ESF operation in a war-torn town.
Later scope from the pitch (vehicles, multiplayer, campaign cutscenes, destructible
environments) is roadmap, not the current build.

### Mission 1: OPERATION TOTAL CONFIDENCE

ESF has been ordered to hold the town square of a war-torn border town "for as long as it
takes", which Command has estimated at "about a wave". Waves of hostiles escalate until Doug
goes down. Objective text, wave banners, kill messages and Command's radio lines are all
deadpan.

## Visual target

Late-afternoon golden light over a dusty, war-damaged town (see `ASHFALL_ART_DIRECTION.md` for
the detailed environment and material direction, which still applies). The HUD is a clean,
restrained, modern-CoD layout in white / off-white with a single warm accent and red for
threats; no arcade neon.
